# Your questions answered, round 2 — 2026-08-20 evening (indoor-nav doc feedback)

## 1. Bluetooth bone-conduction latency — your fear is correct

Measured: standard Bluetooth (SBC codec, which **every Shokz model
uses**) = **257–260 ms, and it jitters**. For head-tracked spatial ticks
that's disqualifying: turn your head at 90°/s and the tick lands ~23°
away from the obstacle — actively wrong during the exact moment you're
scanning. Open-ear makes it worse: you hear the real world at 0 ms, so
the lag is glaring (detection threshold ≈ 30 ms).

**Split the audio into two channels:**
- **Spatial ticks/beacons → wired DIY**: 2 bone-conduction transducers
  bonded to the helmet shell (Dayton BCE-1 $76, or 2× $16.62 generic
  drivers + $5 amp). Zero latency, and the transducers sit at a KNOWN
  pose relative to the IMU — a consumer band shifts independently and
  breaks the spatial math.
- **Speech/menus → any Bluetooth open-ear is fine** (260 ms doesn't hurt
  sentences). Soundcore V30i $44.99 if you want one.
Full guide: [[feasibility-pack-2026-08-20]] §6.

## 2. What are IR projectors (.lumen's)?

Infrared floodlights/pattern projectors — they light the scene with
wavelengths humans can't see, so the cameras can see in darkness (same
idea as security-camera night vision). .lumen ships 2 IR *laser*
projectors, which also project a dot pattern that gives their cameras
texture to compute depth from on blank walls. Our version is already in
IDEA-BANK §C: cheap IR LED floods (~$10) would let our camera work at
night — and note our ToF is ALREADY active-IR, so unlike Glide we don't
go blind in the dark; only the camera does.

## 3. "Glide = stereo + mmWave" — what I meant

- **Stereo-depth cameras**: two cameras a known distance apart; depth
  computed from parallax, like your two eyes. Glide's FAQ confirms two
  stereo-depth cameras in the handle. Passive (needs light — hence
  Glide's darkness limitation).
- **mmWave radar**: millimetre-wavelength radio sensing — works through
  rain, fog, darkness; sees moving things extremely well. I flagged it
  in the indoor-nav doc as the rain-immune sensor CLASS in that product
  space (and as our deferred option — cheap modules have no azimuth
  resolution, i.e. they say "something at 2 m" but not where). Glide
  itself is camera-based per its FAQ; treat any mmWave attribution to
  Glide as unconfirmed.

## 4. How does biped NOA work?

Shoulder-worn harness (like a collar draped over both shoulders): **3
ultra-wide cameras** (~170° combined), an NVIDIA Jetson-class computer
on the harness, and **self-driving-derived software** — it tracks every
moving object, predicts each one's trajectory a few seconds out, and
warns ONLY about things on a collision course with you (their pitch:
"like a self-driving car for pedestrians"). Output = 3D spatial beeps
through headphones from the direction of the threat + GPS turn-by-turn.
That trajectory-prediction filter is their one great idea and it's
already in our IDEA-BANK (§D "trajectory-prediction alerts" — ByteTrack
velocity + own motion → per-object TTC). Caveat: biped.ai's domain has
been dead in all our checks — the company may be defunct; the idea
survives.

## 5. "Fleet-based AI that gets smarter" — what it means, how we build it

Their claim decoded: every deployed Glide uploads real-world sensor data
(especially safety-critical moments), models retrain centrally, better
weights ship back over-the-air to all devices — a **data flywheel**
(more devices → more data → better model → more sales). The $30/mo
subscription funds this loop. Full breakdown:
[[glidance-deep-dive-2026-08-20]].

**Our version at fleet-size 1** (cheap, real, portfolio-worthy):
1. The own-data capture loop already planned (auto-label → correct →
   nightly fine-tune) IS this flywheel.
2. **Log every intervention as a training label**: F8-hush, stumbles,
   overrides — save the surrounding 10 s of camera+ToF+IMU.
3. A "flag that" voice/tap bookmark for hard moments in the field.
4. Design the telemetry log format NOW (frames + ToF + IMU + what the
   system said + what you did) — that schema is what turns one helmet
   into a fleet later.

## 7. Camera resolution for Apple-style signage (your instinct is right)

Already measured: the fisheye delivers ~16 px/° — a 15 cm sign character
is 8 px at 20 m (unreadable; need ~24–30 px). That's WHY the
implementation guide's #1 hardware call is a **second narrow (~38–50°)
global-shutter camera** — it reads text at 10–30 m, does the last-metre
fine guidance, and yes: signage needs OCR models (PARSeq, 15 ms/crop)
running alongside YOLO. All spec'd in
[[implementation-guide-2026-08-20]] + [[transit-headsign-ocr-2026-08-20]].

## 8. "Find Heinz" — your text-search idea is genuinely good

Logged in IDEA-BANK as **find-by-text**: user names a word, we run text
spotting over the video and beacon toward any hit. This sidesteps the
SKU database war entirely — no product catalog, just "does this word
appear in view." Realistic constraints: works at ~0.5–2 m on shelf
labels with the fisheye (farther with camera #2); needs the scanning
head sweep you'd naturally do. Same OCR stack as signage/labels — one
engine, three features (labels, signage, find-by-text).
