# Abandonment + low-tech research — 2026-08-18 (sub-agent findings, condensed verbatim)

Part of the daily-life research fleet. Load-bearing conclusions for the helmet:

1. **Phillips & Zhao 1993 (n=227): 29.3% of devices completely abandoned;
   MOBILITY AIDS are the worst category; abandonment peaks in YEAR ONE and
   after year five.** #1 predictor: lack of consideration of user opinion in
   selection. Counterintuitive: EASY procurement predicts abandonment (low
   investment = discard). Any success claim must survive 12+ months.
2. **Only 29% of 140 reviewed ETA studies included blind participants** — the
   field systematically commits the top abandonment error.
3. **The only literature-verified white-cane gap = head/torso-height
   obstacles.** Santos 2020 head-to-head (n=10 blind + 31 blindfolded):
   electronic cane's ONLY advantage was hanging obstacles (79% detected) and
   it made users significantly SLOWER. Target exactly the overhead gap,
   silent otherwise.
4. **Derek Riemer (blind SWE), "Some Thoughts on Smart Canes":** "there
   aren't any problems that need solved [on the cane]"; "I don't want my
   cane talking to me when I'm crossing a street"; **"The shut-up mode
   should be the default and I should have to tell it to talk. This is very
   critical."** "My phone already has GPS."
5. **Don't touch the tactile channel the cane owns** — users confused device
   vibration with cane feedback (Santos 2020). (Our temple motors are far
   from the cane hand — good.)
6. **Weight is physics, not preference** (Daniel Kish): mass destroys the
   cane's tactile channel; "the lighter the touch, the more information."
   Smart canes at 3 lb vs <1 lb: repetitive-strain complaints (Stanford
   Daily blind author: "My cane isn't smart, but I don't need it to be").
7. **Low-tech beats high-tech in daily life** (Sci Reports 2025, n=16): ALL
   16 used tactile labeling; hierarchy = try independently → tactile aid →
   digital LAST. "I'd rather rely on strategies that work pretty much every
   time." Apps abandoned because "too long to learn and too long to even
   pull up." Design thesis: **"not compete with but complement existing
   tactile strategies."**
8. **Adoption is behavioral, not economic**: guide dogs are FREE to users
   ($50k value) and reach only ~2%. Cane use itself is only 2–8% of the VI
   population.
9. **Stigma/aesthetics drive abandonment**: modern aesthetics without
   blindness symbolism accepted; devices that "advertise" disability
   rejected.
10. **Don't market on deficit** (Kish): fear-based "what the blind person
    can't do" framing is the reputational trap.
