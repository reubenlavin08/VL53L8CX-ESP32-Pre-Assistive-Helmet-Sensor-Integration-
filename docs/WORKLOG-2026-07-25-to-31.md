## 1. Camera bring-up

**Problem.** Camera enumerated but every frame was pure black.

**Root cause.** Two separate things. First the lens was physically covered. Then, once
uncovered, **1920×1080 MJPEG still returned an all-zero payload** while 1280×720 and
YUY2 640×480 worked perfectly.

**Investigation (the honest version).** My first "proof" was wrong: I used
`CAP_PROP_CONVERT_RGB=0` to grab a "raw" payload and found 6.2 MB of zeros with no JPEG
marker. **That test was invalid** — OpenCV's DirectShow backend pins output to RGB24 and
never hands back raw MJPEG, so I was reading uninitialised memory, not the device. The
load-bearing evidence turned out to be **`ffmpeg -c:v copy`**, which writes the camera's
own JPEG bytes to disk with no decoder in the path: at 1080p it never produced a single
file, at 720p it worked. That rules out every decode-side explanation.

**Real cause.** The module's firmware declares **uncompressed bitrates inside its MJPEG
descriptors** — 1080p MJPEG reports `dwMaxBitRate 995328000`, which is exactly
`1920×1080×2 bytes×30×8`, the YUY2 figure copy-pasted. Per UVC 1.5 §4.3.1.1.1 the host
reserves bandwidth *from that advertised figure*, so it tries to reserve ~10× what is
needed and allocation fails. The stream starts and delivers nothing. Linux patches this
exact defect with `UVC_QUIRK_FIX_BANDWIDTH`; Vimicro's other USB IDs carry that quirk in
`uvc_driver.c` and **ours (0x0AC8:0x0346) was never added. Windows has no equivalent.**

**Fix.** Capture at **MJPG 1280×720**. Costs nothing real — detectors downscale to ~640
anyway and the ToF is a 4×4 grid.

**Lessons.**
- **`-c:v copy` is the honest test on Windows.** It bypasses every decoder. Do not trust
  `CAP_PROP_CONVERT_RGB=0` on the DSHOW backend to give raw bytes.
- Throughput was never the limit: USB 2.0 HS allows 24.6 MB/s per isochronous endpoint;
  1080p30 MJPEG needs only ~12.4 MB/s. **A wrong number in a descriptor broke it, not physics.**

## 2. There is no datasheet for this camera

**Problem.** We had been carrying "~109° H / 67° V" as if it were spec.

**Root cause.** It was an **extrapolation from sibling products**. HBVCAM publishes no
datasheet for the "WA" variant at all — verified negative, it's a reseller SKU.

**Fix.** Ground truth from the device's own UVC descriptors
(`ffmpeg -f dshow -list_options`), and the real FOV from calibration.

**Lesson — now a standing rule.** *"For every single piece of hardware we work with you
must always have the official spec sheet before making any inferences."* Where none
exists, say so explicitly and get ground truth from the device — never silently
substitute a sibling's datasheet.

## 3. The live-capture tool kept freezing

**Problem.** The checkerboard capture tool froze the instant it detected a board.

**Root causes — three, stacked.**
1. **A console window stuck in QuickEdit "select" mode.** On Windows, clicking inside a
   console *pauses the program*; the next `print()` blocks forever. The script printed on
   every capture → froze on first detection. **This was the main one.**
2. **DirectShow frame-queue backup.** When detection made the loop slower than 30 fps,
   frames queued in the driver and every `read()` returned an older frame — it looked
   frozen while actually showing the past.
3. Full-resolution corner detection plus synchronous PNG encoding (~200 ms) in the loop.

**Fixes.** Launch with `pythonw.exe` (no console to click into); a reader thread that
keeps only the newest frame; PNG encoding on a worker thread.

**Then I over-corrected:** dropping detection to quarter resolution made the frame
320×180, where the board's squares were too small to detect at all. Reverted.

**Lesson.** "It froze" had nothing to do with compute — the user's instinct that a GPU
should fix it was reasonable but wrong. `findChessboardCorners` is CPU-only in OpenCV and
there is no CUDA version. **Diagnose the block before optimising the work.**

## 4. Abandoned the hand-rolled live tool

**Problem.** The interactive capture tool kept needing fixes.

**Research verdict.** **No free turnkey Windows GUI does fisheye + live capture.**
OpenCV's own `interactive-calibration` app is **pinhole only** (verified: zero fisheye
references in its source). ROS `camera_calibration` has good fisheye support but needs
ROS. Kalibr is ROS-bound, BabelCalib is MATLAB. caliscope is free and does real fisheye
but wants ChArUco and video files. calib.io Calib is the only true GUI match — paid, with
a 14-day trial.

**Fix — delete the hard part.** `record_calib.py` records a video with a countdown and a
live preview and **no detection in the loop**; `extract_from_video.py` does detection
offline where nothing can race. The solver was never the janky part — `cv2.fisheye` is
what every tool wraps.

**Lesson.** When a UI keeps breaking, remove the UI rather than harden it.

## 5. Calibration — first attempt failed, second succeeded

**Attempt 1: RMS 5.52 px, garbage output** (180° diagonal FOV, optical centre 58 px off).

**Root cause.** The board filled only **0.6–2.4%** of the frame in every view — roughly
10 px per square. Corners cannot be localised at that scale and the distortion model had
nothing constraining it.

**Fix.** Minimum-fill and minimum-sharpness filters in the extractor, and hold the board
closer. *(My first threshold, 8%, rejected every frame — a letter-size board on a 140°
lens only reaches 1.3–7.4% at normal distances. Measured the actual distribution and set
it to 3%.)*

**Attempt 2: RMS 0.61 px**, then **0.30 px** after dropping the two worst-reprojection
frames (1.18 and 0.80 px).

### ✅ Final calibration — `camera/calibration_720p.npz`

```
MJPG 1280x720, cv2.fisheye (Kannala-Brandt), 19 views, RMS 0.3006 px

fx = 669.823   fy = 670.075        cx = 620.953   cy = 335.169
D (k1..k4) = [-0.079359, -0.006644, 0.016116, -0.007523]

MEASURED FOV:  horizontal 119.58°   vertical 63.12°   diagonal 144.02°
```

**Why we trust it.** The diagonal came out **144.0° against a claimed 140°** — an
independent check the solver never saw. `fx` and `fy` agree to 0.04%, so pixels are
square and the fit is clean. The earlier 158.8° was the two bad frames.

**Consequence.** We had been assuming ~109° horizontal. It is **119.6°** — about 10°
wider. That changes how much of the camera view the ToF fan covers.

**⚠️ K is resolution-dependent. Always capture at 1280×720 or recalibrate.**

## 6. Mount geometry locked

- **ToF ×2: ±22.5° yaw, 22.5° down.** 45° apart → 90° combined, edge to edge. *Not* 45°
  off-centre each — that opens a 45° blind hole dead ahead.
- **Camera: 0° yaw, 22.5° down.**

**Why the camera also sits at 22.5°** (`docs/camera_tof_overlap.png`): it centres the ToF
fan in the frame with **symmetric 9.1° margins**, so depth occupies the middle 14–86% of
the image, away from the edges where fisheye distortion is worst and our calibration is
least constrained. **A 20° tilt is worse, not better** — it drops the bottom margin to
6.6° and pushes the ToF band to 90% of frame height. The user's instinct (keep clear of
the bottom edge) was right; the direction was inverted.

**Vertical coverage at 22.5° down**, sensor at 1.6 m (`docs/tof_tilt_chart.png`): fan
spans 0°–45° below horizontal, floor covered from 1.6 m out, and at 2 m+ a person is
seen floor-to-1.6 m. 22.5° is the largest downtilt that keeps the top ray horizontal.

**Known gaps:** nothing above 1.6 m; nothing below 0.60 m at 1 m range.

## 7. Sensor research

**SATEL-VL53L8 — ST publishes no dimensioned drawing.** The Gerbers and STEP model are
the only mechanical sources. **Downloaded ST's STEP and measured it directly:**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| PCB | **51.500 × 19.500 × 1.578 mm** *(caliper said 51 × 19 — agrees)* |
| VL53L8CX package | X 4.910–7.940, Y 6.550–12.950, top 1.750 above PCB |
| **Pins protrude BELOW PCB** | **10.060 mm** ← drives pod clearance |
| Tallest above PCB | 8.482 mm |

**Cross-check:** the datasheet's optical axis (1.615 mm from the long edge, 0.100 mm off
package centre) is reproduced exactly by the STEP placement. Two independent ST sources
agreeing.

**The board snaps apart** (documented perforations, mini-PCB). *User chose to keep the
full board — only a few grams.*

**AN5939 window rules** (only if a window is ever used): air gap < 0.5 mm without a
gasket, gap + glass < 1.5 mm, two circular apertures **concentric with the optical
centres**. **We use open apertures instead** — simplest compliant option.

### ❌ HC-SR04 is a poor overhead detector

- A **bare branch reflects ~30 dB weaker than a flat plate** and fills 3–5% of the beam at
  2–3 m. **No source demonstrates reliable single-branch ranging.**
- Even flat lintels are marginal: the specular window for a 30 cm lintel is **±1.6°**, and
  *larger* flat surfaces are narrower still. Head pitch while walking swings them in and out.
- **Two sensors = 8.3 Hz each** after the mandatory 60 ms round-robin. Crosstalk's failure
  mode is a **false SHORT reading** — the worst kind on an alert device.
- The "15° beam" is marketing; no datasheet gives a dB point. Usable cone ~30°.
- Classic HC-SR04 **won't run at 3.3 V** and its ECHO is 5 V into a non-5 V-tolerant
  ESP32. **Use HC-SR04P / RCWL-1601** — 3.0–5.5 V, software-identical.
- Temperature: 340 m/s hardcoded ⇒ **±15 cm at 4 m** across Vancouver's range.

**Better option: LD19 / STL-19P 2D lidar (~$70–99), scan plane VERTICAL.** A sweeping
plane physically cannot miss a horizontal branch by pointing between it — exactly the
ultrasonic failure mode. *User chose HC-SR04 as a cheap first pass, which is fine — just
don't build the head-protection promise on it.*

## 8. CAD

CadQuery 2.8.0. Parametric Python → STEP (editable in Onshape/SOLIDWORKS) + STL.

**`cad/components.py`** — every component, all dimensions tagged
`[ST-STEP]` / `[DS]` / `[CALIPER]` / `[TBD]`:
- `tof_satel_vl53l8` — exact, from ST's model
- `camera_hbv1716wa` — 38 × 38 confirmed by caliper; hole pitch, depth, lens Ø still `[TBD]`
- `hcsr04` — datasheet; can pitch `[TBD]` (scaled off a drawing, never dimensioned)

**`cad/sensor_pod.py`** — the precision part. **132 × 62 × 46 mm, ~60 g in PLA.**

*First revision came out at **359 g** — a solid block. Shelling it to a 2.4 mm wall before
cutting the pockets took it to 60 g with identical outer geometry, so the sensor angles
are untouched. **Always check printed mass on a wearable.***

**Why one part:** Stage 3's calibration holds only while the ToF and camera cannot move
relative to each other. Everything optical lives in this one body; the helmet interface
is separate, so changing helmets never invalidates the optics.
