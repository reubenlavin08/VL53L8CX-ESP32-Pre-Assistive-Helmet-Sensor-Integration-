# Your questions — glass CV, text UX, ILD calibration, audio blending — 2026-08-22

## 1. Can computer vision detect glass? Yes — and you already ✅'d it

This is a real, researched CV capability (you approved it 2026-08-20,
QA doc decision #1; easy to lose in the volume). Glass segmentation
networks (GDNet-B and successors in the RGB-D glass-detection
literature) find glass the way humans do — from **context**: frames,
reflections, boundary continuity, what's visible through it. They ship
usable accuracy on the public glass benchmarks.

**Our defense-in-depth against walking into glass (4 layers):**
1. **CV glass segmentation** — camera flags glass-likely regions.
2. **ToF anomaly signature** — glass zones show suspect status codes,
   multi-target returns, sigma spikes in a spatially coherent
   rectangular patch (the TOPGN pattern; prototype pending).
3. **Cross-check** — camera says "wall/door here" + ToF says "nothing
   here" = classic glass; trust the camera, warn.
4. **Behavioral floor** — uncertainty near a suspected pane = slow-down
   warning, not silence. And the cane remains the contact-level backstop
   (your standing rule).
Optional layer 5 someday: $35 ultrasonic ranger — sound reflects 99.99%
off glass. It's IDEA-BANK #36.

## 2. Text recognition — when does it read?

Design (mirrors Apple's Detection Mode + our silence-default):

- **Pull, not push, for reading**: a command — voice later ("read
  what's in my hand"), tap/F-key now — triggers OCR of whatever is
  centered in view. Read-in-hand and find-by-text both work this way.
- **The sign problem you named** ("we don't know when we're looking for
  signs") — solved with a **text-noticed earcon**: text DETECTION
  (cheap, runs continuously) is separate from text READING (expensive,
  on demand). When salient text is present — big, near-center, stable
  for ~1 s — a tiny distinct earcon says "there's readable text here."
  You choose whether to say/press "read it." One chirp, never speech,
  60 s cooldown per text region. Silence-default preserved.
- **Find-by-text mode** inverts it: you name a word; the reader runs
  continuously but stays silent until the word matches → beacon to it.

## 3. The ILD sweep — per person? Works with cheap headsets?

- **Why it exists**: skull left-right crosstalk varies enormously
  between people (~40 dB spread in the literature), so a fixed
  left/right volume law lands differently on every head.
- **But it's OPTIONAL tuning, not a gate.** The Soundscape beacon
  encodes direction primarily by TIMBRE (4 regions), not by subtle
  level differences — it works out of the box on any open-ear
  headset. The sweep only sharpens the stereo pan layer.
- **Reality check on duration**: it's ~2–3 min of "which side is this
  tone on?" — once per person, stored as a profile. Only worth doing
  when we chase precision spatial audio (September, wired pair).
- **Your purchased headsets**: yes, both work today for everything
  built (speech, ticker, beacon). The sweep applies to any of them;
  it just matters least on coarse-region audio.

## 4. Blending beeps + voice — researched AND mostly built

This is the tier architecture that's already running:

| Channel | Carries | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Ticker** (600 Hz blips) | proximity/TTC only | closing < 2 s TTC |
| **Beacon** (musical, key g) | direction to a chosen target | only when guiding |
| **Speech** | identity + hazards + commands | tiered: directive interrupts, caution on change, else silent |
| **Haptics** | direction, urgent | near-field |

Priority: directive speech > caution > beacon > ticker (ticker mutes
during speech; beacon ducks). Nothing describes "everything around
you" — that was the vOICe failure (73 h training, zero adoption).

**Your FPS insight is correct and now logged**: because speech is
event-driven, the detector doesn't need constant full-rate. Plan:
**event-driven detector scheduling** — YOLO at ~2–3 fps idle, boosted
to full rate when (a) ToF sees something new/closing, (b) beacon is
guiding, (c) a query/read command fires. Pairs with foveated ROI
(#38). Saves most of the GPU/battery for the untethered future.
